1. WHAT IS AN AVALANCHE AND WHY IS THIS PROJECT NEEDED?
================================================================================

An avalanche happens when a huge mass of snow breaks off a steep mountain slope 
and rushes downhill at speeds up to 100+ km/h. It can bury mountain highways, 
destroy army bases, and kill skiers or villagers.

Traditionally, mountain experts had to manually hike up risky slopes or look at 
weather reports by hand. 

OUR PROJECT AUTOMATES AND IMPROVES THIS PROCESS:
We built a software platform that combines:
  1. Mountain weather and snow sensor data,
  2. Artificial Intelligence (Machine Learning),
  3. Strict physical safety rules, and
  4. An interactive visual map dashboard,
to tell safety officers: "Warning! This mountain pass has high avalanche danger today!"

3. STEP-BY-STEP FLOW OF WORK
================================================================================

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 1: GATHERING MOUNTAIN DATA (THE INPUTS)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Where do we get our information?
From mountain weather stations (called SNOTEL / AWS) or uploaded CSV files.

The system looks at 7 critical factors:
  1. Slope Angle: Avalanches rarely happen on flat ground. Most happen between 
     30 and 45 degrees steepness.
  2. Fresh Snowfall (Last 24h & 72h): Heavy fresh snow piles weight on top 
     of old snow, making it ready to slide.
  3. Snow Depth & Density: How thick and heavy is the total snowpack?
  4. Temperature: Sudden warming melts ice and lubricates the slide layer.
  5. Wind Speed & Direction: Wind strips snow from one side of the mountain 
     and dumps it as a heavy "snow slab" on the opposite (lee) side.
  6. Aspect: Which direction does the slope face (North, South, East, West)? 
     (This determines how much sunlight or wind it gets).
  7. Elevation: Higher altitudes have colder, harsher conditions.

In our project:
We have a master dataset of 65+ premier world mountains (Himalayas, Alps, 
Rockies, Andes) including Mount Everest Khumbu Icefall, K2, Gulmarg, Zojila, 
Mont Blanc, and Denali.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 2: DATA CLEANING & VALIDATION (MAKING SURE THE DATA IS NOT JUNK)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
In the harsh mountains, sensors often freeze, disconnect, or send corrupt numbers 
(like -999). 

Our system does 3 things before doing any math:
  • Freshness Check: Is the data fresh? If a sensor hasn't updated for more 
    than 6 hours, it marks the station as "STALE" and warns the user.
  • Range Validation: Checks if numbers are realistic (e.g. slope must be 
    between 0 and 90 degrees; temperature cannot be +70 C in snow).
  • Imputation & Scaling: Fills small gaps using median averages and scales 
    the numbers so the AI models don't get confused by big numbers.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 3: THE AI BRAIN (CALCULATING AVALANCHE PROBABILITY)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Now that the data is clean, it is sent to our Machine Learning models:
  • Logistic Regression (fast, transparent baseline)
  • Random Forest (examines complex non-linear combinations of weather)
  • Probability Calibration (CalibratedClassifierCV): Ensures that if the 
    model says "80% risk", it truly represents an 80% real-world likelihood.

The AI analyzes all inputs together and outputs a probability score from 
0.0 (0% risk) to 1.0 (100% risk).


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 4: THE "SAFETY NET" RULES (PHYSICAL HEURISTIC OVERRIDES)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
THIS IS ONE OF THE STRONGEST FEATURES OF OUR PROJECT.
Why can't we trust AI 100% by itself?
Because in safety-critical systems, an AI might have a blind spot and say 
"Low Risk (25%)", but mountain physics says an avalanche is guaranteed!

To protect human lives, we built deterministic physical override rules:
  • RULE 1 (Critical Slope + Heavy Snow): 
    If slope is >= 34 degrees AND new snow is >= 30mm, the system instantly 
    FORCES the risk to HIGH, overriding any lower AI prediction.
  • RULE 2 (Severe Wind Loading): 
    If winds exceed 30 km/h blowing onto lee slopes, the risk level is 
    escalated upwards by one level.
  • RULE 3 (Stale Telemetry Guard): 
    If sensor data is missing or outdated, the system refuses to say "Safe". 
    It displays "DEGRADED / REVIEW REQUIRED".

The final output is classified into clear tiers:
  🟢 LOW RISK (0.0 to 0.40)   -> Safe for normal transit
  🟡 MEDIUM RISK (0.40 to 0.70) -> Caution, monitor conditions closely
  🔴 HIGH RISK (0.70 to 1.00)  -> Hazardous, stay away or close corridor


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 5: THE FASTAPI BACKEND SERVER (THE ENGINE UNDER THE HOOD)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built with Python 3.13 and FastAPI.
Runs locally at: http://127.0.0.1:8000

Why FastAPI?
  • Extremely fast (processes predictions in milliseconds).
  • Serves clean REST API endpoints:
      - /predict: accepts coordinates and weather -> returns risk rating
      - /spatial: calculates risk across an entire mountain grid
      - /telemetry: ingests live sensor readings
      - /health: monitors system status and model readiness
  • Automated Interactive Documentation at http://127.0.0.1:8000/docs.

IMPORTANT ARCHITECTURE RULE:
"The Backend is the Single Source of Truth." 
The frontend browser NEVER calculates risk scores on its own. Everything is 
calculated on the backend so no calculation can ever be faked or go out of sync.


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 6: THE USER DASHBOARD (WHAT THE USER SEES & INTERACTS WITH)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Built with React, TypeScript, and Vite.
Runs locally at: http://localhost:5173

The web dashboard is divided into intuitive, user-friendly tabs:
  1. 🗺️ Leaflet GIS Map: 
     A satellite/topographic interactive map showing mountain passes with 
     Green, Yellow, and Red pins. Clicking any pin shows its full weather 
     and risk breakdown.
  2. 📊 CSV Data Studio: 
     Users can drag-and-drop any CSV file with weather readings, or click 
     presets to run batch predictions for 65+ mountains across the world.
  3. 📈 Weather & Snow Analytics: 
     Clean visual charts showing 24h/72h snowfall, wind loading vs. slope, 
     and temperature trends.
  4. 🛡️ Safety Advisories & Bulletins: 
     Explains the exact reasons behind the warning (e.g. "Critical starting 
     zone slope triggered heuristic safety ratchet").


--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
STEP 7: THE HUMAN OFFICER'S FINAL DECISION (HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
This project is an AI Decision-Support System, NOT an autonomous robot.
It provides clear intelligence and explainability so that human officers 
(Border Roads Organisation, Army mountain commanders, highway authorities, 
or ski patrol chiefs) can make the final call on:
  • Closing a mountain pass
  • Triggering controlled preventative avalanches with explosives
  • Evacuating vulnerable checkpoints

5. HOW TO EXPLAIN THIS IN A 2-MINUTE VIVA / INTERVIEW
================================================================================

If your professor or examiner asks: "Can you explain the workflow of your project?"
You can answer smoothly using these 4 points:

"Our project is an AI-based Avalanche Risk Intelligence System built for 
Smart India Hackathon problem statement SIH260105. 

The workflow has 4 simple stages:

1. INPUT & CLEANING:
   We collect 7 critical mountain weather and terrain parameters: slope angle, 
   fresh snowfall, snow depth, temperature, wind speed, wind direction, and 
   elevation. The system validates the data and ensures sensor telemetry is fresh.

2. AI PREDICTION:
   The cleaned data is fed into calibrated machine learning models (Random Forest 
   and Logistic Regression) which calculate the statistical probability of an 
   avalanche occurring.

3. PHYSICAL SAFETY OVERRIDE (THE UNIQUE HIGHLIGHT):
   Because lives are at stake, we don't rely only on AI. We built deterministic 
   physical rules: for example, if the slope is 34 degrees or steeper and heavy 
   fresh snow has fallen, the system forcefully escalates the risk to HIGH, 
   preventing dangerous false-safe AI errors.

4. USER DASHBOARD:
   The results are sent to our React + Leaflet GIS console on port 5173, where 
   operators can see color-coded maps, snow charts, and batch-test 65+ global 
   mountain passes from a master CSV dataset with one click."
